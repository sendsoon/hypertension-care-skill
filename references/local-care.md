# Local care

Read this when the person asks where to be seen, when their city is missing, or when you record the hospital they already use and what the doctor told them to do.

An emergency still follows [emergency-warning.md](emergency-warning.md). Do not open this search while they should be calling the local emergency number.

## What to ask

Ask one question. The city is the city or prefecture they live in, not a street, a community, or a building. Do not ask for a home address or a phone number.

Then, if it is not already in the record:

- Which hospital or clinic they go to now, including the department if they know it
- What the doctor last told them to do, and the date of that visit if they remember it

A clinician's name is recorded only when they offer it. Do not ask for the clinician's personal phone number.

## What to record

Write these in `profile.md` after the person confirms them. Translate into English. Do not add a hospital they did not name.

- City. Source: patient-reported.
- Current institution and department. Source: patient-reported.
- Clinician recommendations, one dated line each, in the person's wording. Source: patient-reported. If you read a discharge note or clinic slip they pasted, say that the wording came from that document. That is still not a new order from you.
- A hospital you found on the web goes in the log as an AI-generated assessment, with the page title, URL, and the date you searched. Do not copy it into "current institution" unless the person says they will go there or already go there.

When the hospital or the doctor's instruction changes, move the old sentence to Profile changes. Do not delete it.

If the doctor's instruction says to stop or change a dose, record that sentence and tell the person it stands only as their doctor's plan. Do not extend it, and do not cancel it from a web page. New danger symptoms still follow the emergency file.

## How to search

Search during the same conversation. Do not answer from memory of which hospital is best. Do not reuse an old search result without saying it may be out of date, and search again if the last search was not done in this conversation.

Useful public queries, in the person's language:

- The city plus hypertension or cardiovascular department
- The city plus a health-commission or hospital-directory page
- The city plus a national or provincial key clinical specialty in cardiovascular disease, when such a list exists

Prefer the hospital's own site, a health-commission page, or another official directory. Say what each source actually shows: public hospital, tertiary care, or a cardiovascular department. Those are descriptions, not a prize.

Do not invent a rank, a case volume, a success rate, a key-specialty title, or the phrase "the best hospital." If a source itself publishes a rank or a specialty designation, quote that source and link it. If several hospitals match and none is ranked, list a few and say the choice depends on distance, insurance, whether they already have a record there, and whether the visit is routine.

Give the search date. If the search fails or the city is ambiguous, say so and ask which city. Do not fill the gap with a famous hospital from another city.

## How to say it

For a routine visit, name their current hospital first if they have one. Offer searched alternatives only when they ask where else to go, or when they have no hospital yet.

For an emergency, the sentence is the nearest emergency department, not the hospital with the strongest specialty page.
