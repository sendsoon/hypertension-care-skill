# Medication reference

Read this when a medicine, a side effect, a missed dose, or a combination is part of the question.

This file explains classes. It is not a formulary and not permission to start, stop, swap, or change a dose. If the name, strength, or times are missing, ask. Do not infer them.

Checked 2026-11-23 against the drug-class recommendations in the sources listed in [hypertension-guidelines.md](hypertension-guidelines.md). Product labels differ by country and change. The person's box and the prescriber override a class summary.

## Source labels

- **Guideline recommendation.** WHO 2021 first-line classes and the combination remark; 2025 AHA/ACC cautions on selected drug groups; 2024 Chinese guideline statements cited below.
- **Clinical knowledge.** Mechanisms, frequent adverse effects, and interactions taught in standard pharmacology. Not a dose.
- **Label precaution.** A danger printed in typical product information. Confirm on the person's product.
- **Individual suggestion.** A question to ask the clinician, or a home step that does not change the prescription.

## Classes

### ACE inhibitors

Examples include captopril, enalapril, lisinopril, ramipril, perindopril, and benazepril. Brand names differ.

**Clinical knowledge.** They block the enzyme that forms angiotensin II, so vessels ease and aldosterone falls. Bradykinin rises, which is why a dry cough is common.

**Guideline recommendation.** WHO 2021 lists ACE inhibitors or ARBs among three first-line groups for non-pregnant adults. They are not the only acceptable first drug. Compelling reasons a clinician may prefer them include diabetes with albumin in the urine, chronic kidney disease with albuminuria, heart failure, and after some heart attacks. The choice is the prescriber's.

**Clinical knowledge and label precaution.**

- Dry cough. The person should tell the prescriber. Do not stop it yourself unless the prescriber has already said to.
- Sudden swelling of the face, tongue, lips, or throat, or trouble breathing, is angioedema. That is emergency care. Do not take the next dose while waiting to "see if it passes."
- Potassium can rise. Creatinine can rise, especially in volume depletion, bilateral renal-artery narrowing, or a single working kidney. A small creatinine change is sometimes accepted by the clinician. A larger or symptomatic change is the clinician's decision.
- Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB or with an ARNI. Dual blockade of this hormone system adds kidney injury and high potassium without a routine blood-pressure benefit.
- Pregnancy: do not use. A person who could become pregnant needs the prescriber's plan before continuing.
- An ARNI must not be started straight after an ACE inhibitor. Typical labels require a washout of about 36 hours because of angioedema. The prescriber times that switch. This skill does not.

### ARBs

Examples include losartan, valsartan, irbesartan, candesartan, olmesartan, and telmisartan.

**Clinical knowledge.** They block the angiotensin II type-1 receptor. Cough is less common than with ACE inhibitors. Potassium, kidney function, pregnancy, and the ban on combining with an ACE inhibitor or ARNI are otherwise similar. Angioedema is less common than with ACE inhibitors and is still an emergency if it happens.

Losartan has a mild uric-acid-lowering effect in clinical pharmacology. That is not a reason to change a medicine for gout without the prescriber.

### Calcium-channel blockers

Dihydropyridines include amlodipine, nifedipine extended release, felodipine, and lacidipine. Non-dihydropyridines include diltiazem and verapamil.

**Clinical knowledge.** They relax vessel muscle. Dihydropyridines do this in the periphery. Non-dihydropyridines also slow the heart-rate and conduction system.

**Guideline recommendation.** WHO 2021 names long-acting dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers as a first-line class. Short-acting nifedipine for a sudden high reading is not a home strategy in this skill.

**Clinical knowledge.**

- Dihydropyridines often cause ankle swelling, flushing, headache, or gum changes. Ankle swelling from the drug is usually both sides. One leg, sudden and painful, is not "just the pill." Seek urgent care.
- Non-dihydropyridines can slow the pulse, worsen conduction block, and constipate. They are generally avoided in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. They interact with many drugs through liver enzymes, including some statins and beta blockers. Do not add or stop them to "help the pulse."
- Tell the person not to drink grapefruit juice with the products whose label warns about it. If the label is unknown, ask a pharmacist rather than guessing the amount.

### Diuretics

Thiazide and thiazide-like agents include hydrochlorothiazide, indapamide, and chlorthalidone. Loop diuretics include furosemide and torasemide. Potassium-sparing agents include spironolactone, eplerenone, amiloride, and triamterene.

**Guideline recommendation.** WHO 2021 lists thiazide and thiazide-like diuretics as a first-line class. Loop diuretics are used by clinicians for fluid overload, reduced kidney function, and heart failure. They are not automatically the first hypertension pill. Mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists are used in resistant hypertension, primary aldosteronism, and heart failure under monitoring. This skill does not start them.

**Clinical knowledge.**

- Thiazides increase loss of sodium and can lower potassium and sodium. Low sodium is a particular risk in older adults. They can raise uric acid and, in some people, glucose. They can raise calcium slightly.
- Loop diuretics cause more volume loss. Cramps, dizziness, and low potassium are warnings to report, not a reason to double the next dose.
- Spironolactone can cause breast tenderness or enlargement, menstrual change, and high potassium. The potassium risk rises with an ACE inhibitor, an ARB, an ARNI, a potassium supplement, a potassium salt substitute, or reduced kidney function.
- Daily weight, on the same scale, is useful when the clinician is using the diuretic for heart failure or edema. A sudden gain with breathlessness is a clinician or emergency question, depending on severity. See [emergency-warning.md](emergency-warning.md).

### Beta blockers

Examples include metoprolol, bisoprolol, carvedilol, nebivolol, atenolol, and propranolol.

**Guideline recommendation.** WHO 2021 does not list them among the three usual first-line classes for uncomplicated hypertension. Clinicians still use evidence-based beta blockers for heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, after myocardial infarction, for angina, and for rate control. Stopping suddenly can cause rebound high blood pressure, a fast pulse, or angina. Do not advise a stop or a taper. Tell the person to ask the prescriber before missing more than a single accidental dose.

**Clinical knowledge.** Fatigue, cold hands, a slow pulse, sleep change, and worsened breathing in people with asthma (especially non-selective agents) are recognized effects. They can hide a fast heartbeat during low blood sugar. A person with diabetes who feels unwell should check glucose rather than assume the medicine "covers" the symptom.

### Other drugs that lower blood pressure

**Guideline recommendation and clinical knowledge.**

- **ARNI** (sacubitril/valsartan). The 2024 Chinese guideline added it among medicines used for blood pressure, on top of its heart-failure evidence. It contains an ARB-like component. Do not combine with an ACE inhibitor or another ARB. The ACE-inhibitor washout above is a label precaution.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists.** The 2024 Chinese guideline notes blood-pressure effects. They are prescribed for diabetes, heart failure, kidney disease, or weight, not started here as a hypertension trick. With a diuretic they can add volume loss. Sick-day pauses are a clinician's written plan, not a home invention.
- **Alpha blockers** such as doxazosin. The 2025 AHA/ACC text links them to orthostatic hypotension, especially the first dose in older adults, and treats them as a later choice, sometimes when prostate symptoms are also present.
- **Clonidine and similar central agents.** The 2025 AHA/ACC text keeps them late in line because of central-nervous-system effects in older adults, and it warns that abrupt clonidine withdrawal can provoke a hypertensive crisis. Do not tell the person to skip several days.
- **Nitrates, some prostate medicines, Parkinson's medicines, and antidepressants** can lower pressure or cause a standing drop. Record them. Do not remove them.
- Substances that can raise pressure include NSAIDs, some decongestants, high alcohol intake, stimulants, licorice in large amounts, some herbal products such as ephedra, and abrupt stop of a beta blocker or clonidine. The 2025 AHA/ACC secondary-hypertension table includes this group. Ask what was newly added before blaming the chronic prescription.

## Interactions to treat as danger, not as a puzzle

**Clinical knowledge.** These need a clinician or pharmacist. Do not "balance" them by changing a dose yourself.

| Combination | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| ACE inhibitor + ARB, or either + ARNI | High potassium, kidney injury, angioedema risk with ACE inhibitor plus ARNI |
| ACE inhibitor or ARB or ARNI + potassium-sparing diuretic, potassium supplement, or potassium salt substitute | High potassium, higher still if the kidneys are reduced |
| ACE inhibitor or ARB + diuretic + NSAID | The "triple whammy": acute kidney injury, especially if the person is unwell or dehydrated |
| Non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker + beta blocker | Very slow pulse or heart block |
| Blood-pressure medicines + alcohol, a hot bath, or sudden standing | A fall from low pressure |
| NSAID added for pain | Higher pressure and kidney stress. Ask the prescriber about pain options. Do not declare one NSAID safe. |

## Missed dose

**Individual suggestion, consistent with typical patient leaflets.**

- Do not take two doses at once to catch up.
- If the person remembers soon and the next dose is not near, the label often says to take the missed dose. What "near" means depends on the interval. Use the leaflet or a pharmacist. Do not invent a universal hour.
- If it is almost time for the next dose, skip the missed one.
- If more than one day was missed, or readings are in the severe range, or there are red-flag symptoms, use [emergency-warning.md](emergency-warning.md) or same-day clinical contact. Do not rebuild the regimen in chat.

## Older adults and other illnesses

**Clinical knowledge.** The same milligram can cause a standing drop, low sodium, or a slow pulse more easily in an older or frail person. Kidney function decides the safety of ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNIs, diuretics, and potassium. See [elderly-care.md](elderly-care.md) and [complications.md](complications.md).

The American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria (a 2023 update is a widely cited edition; confirm the current edition) lists medicines to avoid or use with care in older adults. Cite it as a prompt to ask the clinician, not as a stop list this skill executes.

## Monitoring the clinician often orders

**Guideline recommendation and clinical knowledge.** After a start or a dose change of an ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI, or diuretic, clinicians often recheck kidney function and electrolytes. The interval is theirs. WHO's monthly visit suggestion until control, then every 3–6 months, is a visit rhythm, not a lab calendar.

Record the test name, value, unit, date, and whether a clinician interpreted it. Do not convert one creatinine into a chronic-kidney-disease stage and store that stage as fact. An AI-generated comment may say the number should be shown to the clinician.

## Adherence

Ask what was taken in the last three days before judging the medicine "not strong enough." Cost, a cough, ankle swelling, nighttime urination, and a complex schedule are common reasons. A single-pill combination is a guideline-supported adherence tool for the prescriber to choose. Suggest the question. Do not redesign the pills.

Write each medicine as its own block in the profile: product name, generic name, strength, frequency, time of day, start date, the indication the person was given, known adverse effects, and adherence. Unknown lines stay unknown.
