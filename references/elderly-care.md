# Older adults

Read this when the person is about 65 or older, takes several medicines, has fallen, has trouble walking, or has memory concerns. Pair it with [hypertension-guidelines.md](hypertension-guidelines.md) and [medication-reference.md](medication-reference.md).

Checked 2026-11-23. Targets below are copied from those guideline notes, not recalculated.

## Targets and frailty

**Guideline recommendation.**

- ISH 2020 optimal track: from 65, office target under 140/90 if tolerated, individualized for frailty and independence.
- ESC 2024: systolic 120–129 for most adults who tolerate it, but more lenient for symptomatic orthostatic hypotension, age 85 or older, moderate-to-severe frailty, or limited life expectancy.
- 2024 Chinese guideline: ages 65–79, under 140/90 and under 130/80 if tolerated; age 80 and older, under 150/90 and under 140/90 if tolerated. Age by itself is not a reason to leave pressure high. Evidence is limited for dependent and institutionalized people.
- 2025 AHA/ACC: for frail older adults or limited life expectancy, the clinician and the patient weigh benefit and harm. Do not compute that trade yourself.

**Individual suggestion.** Ask, "What blood-pressure target did your clinician write down?" If nobody has, the next visit question is the target plus a standing blood pressure, not a new drug.

## Falls, standing pressure, and meals

**Clinical knowledge.** A fall, a broken bone, or a head injury can outweigh a small average difference in home readings. Check sitting and standing readings when the person is older, has diabetes, feels dizzy, or has fallen. ESC 2024's drop of at least 20 mmHg systolic and/or 10 mmHg diastolic at 1 or 3 minutes is the definition to use when you interpret a pair of readings the person actually took. One pair does not diagnose autonomic disease.

Practical steps:

- Rise in stages: sit on the edge of the bed, pump the ankles, then stand while holding the bed.
- Hot baths, alcohol, and the hour after a large meal can drop pressure. Sit to dry off. Do not walk to the toilet in the dark.
- If a reading is low and they are dizzy, they lie down and call for help. They do not take the next dose early.
- Night lights and clearing the path are part of blood-pressure care when medicines can cause a standing drop.

## Many medicines

**Clinical knowledge.** Ask for every product, including pain pills, sleep pills, prostate pills, and herbal sachets. NSAIDs, alpha blockers, and clonidine are frequent problems in older adults. The current AGS Beers Criteria is the list to mention to the clinician. This skill does not discontinue a drug because it appears on that list.

Bring one bag of all current boxes to the next visit. In the record, one block per medicine. "A white pill in the morning" stays unknown until the box is read.

Low sodium from a thiazide can look like confusion or a fall. That is a same-day clinician problem, not a home salt load, especially if heart failure is present.

## Eating and muscle

**Individual suggestion.** Protect protein foods they can chew. Unplanned weight loss, a looser belt, or a new struggle to stand from a chair is frailty risk. Tell the clinician. Do not answer it with a lower-calorie slogan.

Walking, sit-to-stand, and counter holding, as in [lifestyle-management.md](lifestyle-management.md), are the default activity if nothing forbids them. A person who needs a walker should use the walker. Do not suggest jogging.

## Memory and mood

**Clinical knowledge.** Forgetfulness can cause double dosing. A weekly pill box, filled with a family member, is the step when the person agrees. If they cannot remember whether today's dose was taken, they should not take a possible second dose. Mark the uncertainty in the log and ask the clinician.

New confusion is illness until assessed. It is not a cue for a memory supplement. Long-term blood-pressure care is relevant to later cognitive risk, and it is not a promise to reverse memory loss.

Hearing and eyesight change how instructions land. Use one step, then check what they heard. Write the step in the log in short English sentences the next caregiver can translate back.

## Caregivers

Record the caregiver by role (spouse, adult child, aide), not by legal name, address, or phone. If the caregiver manages medicines, say so. Do not collect the caregiver's contact details.

A family argument about salt is common. Give the household one visible change, such as cooking without salt and measuring at the table, rather than a ban on shared meals.

## What not to do

- Do not start a "low dose" of anything.
- Do not stop a fall-associated drug in the chat.
- Do not set a 120 systolic goal because a trial included older volunteers.
- Do not ignore chest pain, a fall with head injury, or one-sided weakness because the person is old and "often dizzy." Use [emergency-warning.md](emergency-warning.md).
