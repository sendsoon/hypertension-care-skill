# Follow-up strategies

Read this when deciding what to watch, when to suggest a visit, or how to write the log so the next chat continues cleanly.

## Visit rhythm from guidelines

**Guideline recommendation.**

- WHO 2021 suggests monthly contact after a medicine is started or changed, until the target is reached, then every 3–6 months. The certainty attached to that interval is low.
- ISH 2020 aims for control within about 3 months on its essential track.
- The 2024 Chinese guideline discusses reaching the office target over about 4 to 12 weeks for most adults, faster if young with a short history, slower if older, longstanding, comorbid, or poorly tolerant.
- These are clinic rhythms. This skill does not book them and does not decide that a target has been reached.

**Individual suggestion.** If the person has no clinician visit planned and readings are not in the emergency range, suggest they ask for a visit rather than inventing the week. If readings are in the severe range or red-flag symptoms exist, ignore the routine interval and use [emergency-warning.md](emergency-warning.md).

## What a useful home series looks like

**Individual suggestion, aligned with the measurement section of the guidelines file.**

- Same arm, same validated upper-arm cuff, seated, after 5 quiet minutes.
- Two readings in the morning before medicine and two in the evening, for 7 days, when the question is "what is my usual pressure?" Drop the first day if the clinician uses that convention; if you do not know their convention, average the 7 days and say that you did.
- Bring the list, not a single best or worst number.
- Add standing readings on one morning of that week if the person is older, dizzy, or has fallen.
- Write symptoms, missed doses, a salty meal, a poor night, and pain pills. Those notes stop a false conclusion.

One afternoon of three perfect readings does not prove control. One bad morning does not prove failure. Say that in the risk section.

## What to track over months

Update the profile only when the fact is confirmed. Keep the series in the logs.

| Item | Why it is here | Source label to use |
|---|---|---|
| Home series and pulse | Usual pressure, not one spike | Patient-reported |
| Orthostatic pair | Fall and overtreatment risk | Patient-reported |
| Medicine list and missed doses | Apparent resistance is often adherence or an NSAID | Patient-reported |
| Weight if heart failure, edema, or a weight goal is in the record | Fluid or unintended loss | Patient-reported |
| Swelling, breathlessness, chest pain, dizziness, cough, ankle swelling | Adverse effects and new disease | Patient-reported |
| Kidney function, electrolytes, glucose, lipids, urine | Safety of ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI, diuretic | Clinician-confirmed when from a report; otherwise patient-reported numbers with the date |
| ECG or echocardiogram conclusions | Heart structure and rhythm | Only what the report said |
| Clinician's target and any fluid or potassium limit | Stops the skill from importing a population target | Clinician-confirmed only if they are reporting the clinician |
| Mood, sleep, falls, who helps with pills | Whether the plan is livable | Patient-reported |

## Labs and checks to suggest, not to order

**Clinical knowledge.** Suggest the person ask whether they are due for kidney function and electrolytes after a change in an ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI, or diuretic; glucose and lipids if not checked in the last year; urine albumin if diabetes or kidney disease is present; an ECG if never done or if the pulse is newly irregular. The clinician orders these. Do not give a diagnosis from a number the person remembers without units.

An AI-generated assessment may say "this pattern is worth showing to the clinician." It may not say "you have chronic kidney disease stage 3" unless a clinician has said that. Store the stage only as clinician-confirmed in that case.

## How to write the follow-up so the next chat works

Each log entry includes:

- Date and, if known, time
- The question in the person's words, translated into English
- Symptoms they reported
- Readings they reported
- Assessment, labeled AI-generated, including uncertainty
- The steps you gave, and any step you refused to give, such as a dose change
- Whether you told them to seek emergency care, same-day care, or routine care
- The one question still open
- The next watch item
- What changed since the previous entry

When they later say what happened, append a new entry. Point it at the old file. Do not edit the old advice into what you wish you had said.

## Review across time

Do a review when they ask, or when the new question depends on the history. Separate repeating situations, steps that helped or did not, confirmed changes, and facts still missing. Do not collapse the log into a new diagnosis.

If the profile and the newest story conflict, ask which is current. Keep the old sentence under Profile changes.
