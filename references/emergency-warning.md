# Emergency and urgent warning

Read this before any other reference when a symptom or a number might be dangerous. The reply is the safety step. Do not finish a routine history first. Record the conversation after the person has the emergency instruction.

This skill's action thresholds are operational safety rules. They are deliberately simple. They are not a new diagnostic category.

Local emergency numbers differ. In mainland China the public medical emergency number is 120. Tell the person to use the local emergency number where they are. Do not tell them to drive themselves if they are dizzy, in chest pain, or neurologically unwell.

## Act now: emergency care

**Clinical knowledge and public stroke-recognition campaigns used by the AHA.** Seek emergency care now, and do not wait for a repeat blood pressure, if any of these are new or sudden:

- Chest pain, pressure, or tightness, especially with cold sweat, nausea, or pain into the arm, neck, jaw, or back
- Trouble breathing, breathing at rest, or a feeling of drowning when lying flat
- Fainting, a seizure, or new confusion
- One-sided face droop, arm or leg weakness, trouble speaking, trouble understanding, sudden vision loss, sudden loss of balance, or a sudden severe unusual headache
- Swelling of the lips, tongue, face, or throat, or a rash with breathing trouble, especially on an ACE inhibitor or ARNI
- Coughing or vomiting blood, black stool, or a serious head injury while taking an anticoagulant
- A possible pregnancy with severe headache, visual change, sudden swelling, or upper abdominal pain

Do not suggest an extra dose, a cold shower, a folk remedy, or waiting until the clinic opens.

## Very high numbers

**Guideline recommendation.** The 2025 AHA/ACC guideline describes severe hypertension as blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg without acute target-organ injury. It says this should be evaluated and treated in a timely outpatient way by starting, restarting, or intensifying oral medicines. It advises against intermittent extra intravenous or oral doses to force an acute drop in hospitalized adults who have this number without organ injury and who were admitted for something else. The 2024 Chinese guideline's grade 3 starts at office blood pressure at or above 180 and/or 110.

**Operational rule for this skill.**

1. If a red-flag symptom above is present at any number, emergency care now.
2. If the person reports systolic at or above 180, or diastolic at or above 120, and no red-flag symptom: have them sit quietly for 5 minutes and measure again with a validated upper-arm cuff, unless they are too unwell to do that safely. If the repeat is still in that range, they need same-day clinical assessment, not a lifestyle essay and not a home drug change.
3. If diastolic is at or above 110 but under 120, and systolic is under 180, with no red-flag symptom: this is already grade 3 in the Chinese classification. Arrange same-day clinician contact. Do not call it a normal fluctuation.
4. Never tell the person to take someone else's medicine or an extra tablet unless that extra-dose plan is already written by their clinician and they are following that written plan. If they have no such plan, they wait for clinical instruction.

A high reading with acute organ injury is a hypertensive emergency. The organ injury, not the chat, decides that. Suspected stroke, heart attack, acute heart failure, aortic disaster, eclampsia, and acute kidney injury belong in emergency care. This skill does not coach minute-by-minute blood-pressure reduction. Hospital targets after stroke can be harmed by over-lowering. That is another reason not to push extra doses at home.

## Same day, not "next month"

Contact a clinician the same day, or urgent care if the clinic is shut, for:

- A repeated reading at or above 180/120 without emergency symptoms
- New fainting or a fall
- New irregular fast heartbeat with dizziness
- Rapidly increasing swelling, or breathlessness on slight effort that is new
- A home reading much lower than usual with confusion, falls, or inability to stand
- Vomiting or diarrhea while on an ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI, or diuretic, if the person cannot keep fluids down
- A missed medicine for more than a day plus readings in the severe range

## What a lower but uncomfortable reading means

**Clinical knowledge.** Dizziness on standing, dim vision, or a measured orthostatic drop (ESC 2024: at least 20 mmHg systolic and/or 10 mmHg diastolic at 1 and/or 3 minutes) is a reason to sit or lie down, then tell the clinician. It is not a reason to stop all medicines in chat. If the person cannot stay conscious, is injured, or has chest pain, use emergency care.

A single comfortable reading such as 128/76 is not a reason to stop treatment.

## After the safety step

If the person is going to emergency care, the reply ends with what to take with them: a written medicine list, the monitor if easy to carry, and allergy information. Do not delay departure to build the list.

When the episode is over, record the symptom, the numbers, the time, and the advice as patient-reported plus an AI-generated assessment. Do not record a diagnosis the hospital has not made.
